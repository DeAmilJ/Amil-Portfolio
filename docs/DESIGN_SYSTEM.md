# Amil Portfolio Design System

## Vision

An editorial-inspired portfolio that feels like a curated museum exhibition.

The portfolio should communicate strategic thinking, strong taste, and visual storytelling.

---

## Core Principles

1. Scroll first.
2. Contents is the only flat section.
3. Every other section uses depth, layering, texture, and scale.
4. Imagery should be large and bold.
5. Typography creates hierarchy.
6. Interactions must support the story.
7. Every project introduces its own visual identity.
8. Avoid unnecessary clicking.
9. Every section should have one memorable hero moment.
10. Nothing should feel like a template.

## Experience Philosophy

This portfolio should feel like an immersive editorial publication
and a curated museum exhibition.

The viewer is guided through the work in a deliberate sequence.

Primary experience:
- Scroll continuously through the issue.
- Allow the story to unfold in its intended order.
- Use pacing, whitespace, imagery, and transitions to guide emotion.

Secondary experience:
- The Contents page provides direct navigation.
- Visitors may skip to a specific feature or section.
- Every Contents entry uses smooth-scroll navigation.

Cover language:
- "Scroll for the curated experience."
- "Open Contents to explore freely."

---

## Editorial Rhythm

The website should alternate between:

- Expansive hero moments
- Quiet editorial spreads
- Full-bleed photography
- Detailed archive views
- Short strategic statements
- Large areas of intentional whitespace

Not every screen should contain multiple elements.

Some moments should feature:
- One image
- One sentence
- One question
- One highlighted insight

Whitespace creates pause, importance, and anticipation.

---

## Material Language

Texture should create physical depth without reducing readability.

Shared materials:
- Natural paper grain
- Vellum overlays
- Transparent tape
- Pencil and graphite marks
- Soft ink imperfections
- Subtle folds and worn edges
- Printed photography
- Archival labels
- Highlighter marks
- Sticky notes

Texture must feel integrated into the composition,
not placed over it as decoration.

Textured backgrounds should maintain sufficient contrast.

## Studio Archive

Each project includes a Studio Archive documenting
the evolution from initial thought to final brand world.

Suggested sequence:

1. The Beginning
2. Initial Question
3. Research and Observations
4. Early Notes
5. Naming Exploration
6. Visual References
7. Sketches
8. Rejected Routes
9. Strategic Breakthrough
10. Refinement
11. Final Identity
12. Brand Experience

Archive materials may include:
- Notebook scans
- Rough thumbnails
- Crossed-out ideas
- Early logos
- Type tests
- Color experiments
- Packaging sketches
- Moodboard fragments
- Strategic notes
- Final applications

Do not fabricate process work.

Reconstructed material must be labeled honestly,
such as "Revisited concept notes" or
"Recreated from the original development process."

## Archived Directions

Rejected ideas should demonstrate decision-making.

Each archived direction should explain:

- What the idea attempted to communicate
- What worked visually
- Why it was not selected
- What was learned from it
- How it influenced the final direction

Possible labels:
- Archived Direction
- Early Route
- Not Selected
- Study No. 02
- Development Draft

Avoid presenting rejected work as failure.
It is evidence of refinement.

## Annotation System

Annotations must communicate thinking, not decoration.

### Highlighter

Use for:
- Key research findings
- Repeated audience language
- Strategic discoveries
- Important contradictions
- Final positioning insights

### Sticky Notes

Use for:
- Questions
- Decisions
- Turning points
- Observations
- Personal creative notes

### Handwriting

Use sparingly for:
- Emotional reactions
- Quick reminders
- Questions asked during development
- Short curator comments

Examples:
- "This needs more warmth."
- "How should she feel here?"
- "Too clinical."
- "Repeated across several responses."
- "This became the turning point."

---

## Lighting and Depth

Depth should come from realistic layering and light.

Use:
- Soft directional shadows
- Subtle paper overlap
- Vellum transparency
- Localized spotlights
- Gentle ombré lighting
- Foreground and background separation
- Occasional parallax at very low intensity

Avoid:
- Heavy drop shadows
- Glowing UI elements
- Neon gradients
- Excessive blur
- Floating objects without visual logic

Ombrés should resemble light moving across a physical page,
not digital gradient backgrounds.

Spotlights should reveal important work
as though it were displayed in a gallery.

---

## Page Transitions

Transitions should resemble movement through a book,
archive, or exhibition.

Possible transition behaviors:
- A page lifting gently
- Vellum passing over the screen
- A photograph sliding into alignment
- A new spread revealing itself beneath the previous one
- A spotlight moving from one object to another
- An archive sheet being pulled forward

Motion should feel:
- Smooth
- Slow enough to register
- Fast enough not to interrupt reading
- Consistent
- Purposeful

Avoid:
- Aggressive zooming
- Bouncing
- Spinning
- Excessive horizontal movement
- Animation on every object
- Scroll hijacking

The user must always remain in control of scrolling.

---

## Motion Timing

Suggested durations:

- Micro-interactions: 150–250ms
- Text and image reveals: 400–700ms
- Editorial transitions: 700–1100ms
- Major chapter transitions: 900–1400ms

Use calm easing curves.

Animations should run once when entering the scene
unless interaction requires repetition.

Respect reduced-motion preferences.

---

## Image Direction

Photography should feel editorial rather than promotional.

Prioritize:
- High-resolution imagery
- Strong composition
- Material details
- Human emotion
- Close crops
- Varied scale
- Intentional lighting
- Product context
- Process documentation

Avoid repeating the same mockup several times.

Each image should contribute new information:
- Atmosphere
- Product
- Detail
- Process
- Scale
- Emotion
- Application

## Contents Page

The Contents page is the only intentionally flat section.

Purpose:
- Orient the viewer
- Reveal the issue structure
- Offer direct navigation
- Provide relief between the textured cover and features

It should use:
- Clean background
- Strong typographic hierarchy
- Minimal or no layered texture
- Page or section numbers
- Clickable entries
- Smooth-scroll links

Suggested language:

"Experience the issue in sequence by continuing to scroll."

"Looking for something specific?
Choose a feature below."

---

## Navigation

Persistent navigation should remain visually quiet.

Possible elements:
- Current section number
- Current feature name
- Contents button
- Back to top
- Scroll progress indicator

Navigation may reduce or disappear during immersive spreads,
but direct access to Contents must remain available.

Keyboard navigation must remain functional.

---

## Accessibility

The immersive experience must remain usable.

Requirements:
- Strong text contrast
- Readable body sizes
- Descriptive image alt text
- Visible keyboard focus states
- Semantic heading order
- Reduced-motion support
- Functional experience without animation
- No essential information placed only inside images
- No interactions dependent solely on hovering
- Mobile alternatives for cursor-based effects

Art direction must never prevent comprehension.

---

## Quality Standard

Every section should answer at least one of these questions:

- What problem was being solved?
- What did the research reveal?
- What decision was made?
- Why was that decision made?
- How did the idea evolve?
- What should the viewer feel?
- How does the final design express the strategy?

A beautiful spread without strategic meaning should be revised.
## Avoid

- Generic portfolio cards
- Flat project sections
- Heavy shadows
- Glassmorphism
- Neon gradients
- Autoplay
- Decorative animation without purpose
